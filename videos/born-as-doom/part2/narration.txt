So you leave. You walk across the world, until the roads turn into mountains, and the mountains turn into snow. High in the Himalayas, you find an order of monks who have been waiting for you. They teach you discipline. They teach you sorcery. And when you ask them to help you forge a suit of armor, they do not ask why.

Here is the most Doom thing you will ever do. The monks pull your new mask out of the forge, still glowing orange. They tell you to wait for it to cool. You do not wait. You press it onto your face while it is still burning. Because pain is temporary. And you have decided you will never show the world your face again.

Now you go home.

Latveria has not changed. The Baron still sits in the castle. The villagers are still hungry. Your people are still living in wagons. But you have changed. You walk up the same hill your father was dragged up, alone, in green and iron, and the soldiers who used to hunt you open fire. The bullets bounce off. One by one, the soldiers drop their rifles. By midnight, the Baron is gone. By morning, you are King.

So what is daily life like, as Doom, in Latveria?

You wake up at five. Not because you need to. Because the robots you built to wake you up are programmed never to let you sleep in. You eat breakfast alone, in a dining hall big enough for two hundred people. Every chair is empty except yours.

Then you go to work. And here is the twist nobody expects. Latveria is actually doing great. There is no crime, because every criminal knows exactly who they would have to explain themselves to. There is no hunger, because you rebuilt every farm with machines from your mother's notebooks. The trains run on time. The streets are clean. Every child gets free school. Your people are safer than anywhere in Europe.

There is just one small catch. You cannot leave. Nobody criticizes the King. And somewhere in the castle, a hundred robot copies of you are watching, listening, and reporting back. Your people are fed, safe, and very, very quiet.

In the afternoons, you build. A time machine in the basement. Robots in the armory. And a giant statue of yourself in the town square, because, well, you are Doom. Then, at night, you sit alone in your lab and try, one more time, to reach your mother.

You never manage it.

And sometimes, late at night, you take off the mask, look at your face in the dark window, and realize the scar is still small. It was always small. Reed was right about the equation. The only person who ever really destroyed your face... was you.

Here is the twist. You got everything your mother promised. The castle. The country. The power. Nobody will ever hurt you again. But nobody will ever really know you, either. You traded every person who could have loved you for a country that is afraid of you.

So be honest. If you were born as Victor Von Doom, would you take the mask? Or would you have listened to Reed?
