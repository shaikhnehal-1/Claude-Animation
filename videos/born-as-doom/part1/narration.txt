What if you were born as Victor Von Doom? Not the armor. Not the throne. Not the army of robots wearing your face. Not yet. Tonight, you are just a baby, screaming inside a wagon, on the coldest night of the year, in a tiny mountain country called Latveria.

Outside, a storm is tearing the sky open. Lightning shows you the castle on the hill. Up there, the Baron sleeps warm behind stone walls. Down here, your people sleep in wagons, because nobody in this country wants them anywhere else.

Your father is a healer. Your mother is something else. The villagers whisper that she talks to things in the dark, and the dark talks back. They are right. And one day, everything she promised to the dark... will come looking for you.

But not tonight. Tonight, she holds you close, looks up at that castle, and makes you a quiet promise. One day, my son, that castle will be yours.



You are four years old when your mother dies. Nobody tells you how. The adults just stop saying her name. Your father tells you she is resting. You know that is a lie, because you heard the screaming, and you saw the green fire through the cracks of the wagon door.

You are eight when the Baron's wife gets sick. His soldiers drag your father up the hill to save her. He tries. He really tries. But she dies anyway, and the Baron needs someone to blame. So that night, your father wraps you in his coat, and you run. Through the snow. Through the trees. Torches behind you, dogs barking, soldiers shouting your name. Your father carries you until his legs stop working. In the morning, you wake up warm, because he gave you his coat. He is not warm. He will never be warm again.

So here is the first rule of your life. Kindness got your father killed. Power would have saved him.

You go back to the wagon, open your mother's old trunk, and find her books. Spells. Symbols. Machines drawn in the margins that should not be possible. A normal kid would be scared. You are eight years old, alone, and furious. So you read every page. Twice.

By twelve, you are building things nobody in Latveria has ever seen. You fix rifles for the camp. You build a little robot out of scrap that walks across the table and bows. And you start doing something the Baron's soldiers really do not like. You steal from them. Food, medicine, metal. Every time they search the camp, they find nothing, because you built hidden compartments into every wagon. The soldiers start calling you a ghost. You start calling yourself something else.

Then, one day, a letter arrives from America. A university has heard about the Latverian boy genius. They are offering you a full scholarship. For the first time in your life, someone is offering you a door instead of a wall.

New York is loud, bright, and full of people who have never been cold a single night in their lives. In your first week, you meet your roommate. A tall, friendly, annoyingly smart guy named Reed Richards. He wants to be your friend. You do not want friends. You want results. Because you have a secret project, and it is the reason you came here. A machine that can reach into the place where your mother went. You are going to bring her back.

Reed looks at your blueprints and finds a mistake. One tiny number, in one tiny equation. He tells you. You tell him to mind his own business. Big mistake. The biggest of your life.

The night you turn the machine on, the lab fills with green light. For half a second, you swear you hear her voice. Then the machine explodes, right in your face.

When you wake up, the university has expelled you, Reed is standing at the door looking sorry, and there is a scar across your face. It is small. Barely a line. But when you look in the mirror, you do not see a scar. You see proof that someone like Reed got to be right, and someone like you had to pay for it.
